The mechanism

One flash_loan whose request stack does the whole job, with a single health check at the end — so the position is never observably unhealthy. Without it this is a supply → borrow → zap → supply loop that leaves the user liquidatable between passes.

01Flash-borrowfrom the pool
02Zapswap half → LP
03SupplyLP as collateral
04Borrowagainst it
05Health checkonce, at the end

How it is wired

  • The vault is the Blend from and owns one position; a separate zapper is the flash-loan receiver. That split is not cosmetic — with the vault as its own exec_op receiver, Soroban rejects the callback (“Contract re-entry is not allowed”) because the vault is still on the stack inside open_position. Worth flagging for anyone else composing flash_loan.
  • Depositors hold shares of the vault’s Blend position, derived on read from pool.get_positions(vault) rather than an internal ledger. Interest and liquidation socialise pro-rata with nothing to reconcile — an earlier internal-ledger design desynced the moment a liquidation touched the position.
  • SupplyCollateral pulls the LP by transfer_from, so the vault pre-approves the pool before the loan; the non-flash unwind needs authorize_as_current_contract on the Repay transfer, since an allowance does not cover the pool spending as spender.

The parameter test

Testnet started at c_factor 0.90 to exercise the mechanism. We then lowered the LP c_factor to 0.60 — our mainnet intent — and re-ran the flow against it. With l_factor 0.95 on the borrowable, the liquidation boundary is:

boundary = 1 / (1 − c_factor × l_factor) = 1 / (1 − 0.60 × 0.95) = 2.33×

One finding, and it is ours. The vault still carries max_leverage_bps = 30000 (3.0×) from early testing, which sits above the Blend boundary. At 2.49× the vault happily built the request stack and your health check was the only thing that stopped it. Blend behaved exactly as it should — but a vault whose own cap sits above the boundary wastes user gas discovering the limit. Ours comes down to 92% of the boundary before mainnet, so the health check is the backstop rather than the primary control.

Caveat we want on the record: we have not tested at 1.82×. That needs the borrowable’s l_factor at 0.75; ours is 0.95, which is what gives 2.33×. Same arithmetic, different input. Matching it means queueing another set_reserve and waiting another seven days.

What stands between here and mainnet

  • Pool activation and backstop. We read the threshold as bal_blnd⁴ × bal_usdc ≥ 1e25, which on Comet’s 80/20 ratio resolves to a single point: 247,464 BLND + 2,667 USDC ≈ $13.3K. We can fund that. Whether we should stop there is question 1.
  • The oracle. Testnet runs a mock we control. Mainnet needs the fair-LP adapter (invariant plus Reflector, never pool spot or TWAP) and your view on whether a custom per-pool oracle is acceptable — question 3.
  • Reserve timelock. Adding or changing a reserve on a pool that has left Setup costs a week (#1203 InitNotUnlocked). We hit it twice and waited it out both times.

On our side of the line, not yours

  • Per-user isolation. v1 socialises liquidation losses across vault depositors (the Aave model). Acceptable at launch size under an equity cap; not at scale.
  • Leverage cap down to 92% of the boundary, as above.
  • Self-closing unwind. v1 requires the user to bring the repay asset.
  • Third-party security audit of vault, zapper, oracle adapter and liquidator.

Questions

Q1

Mainnet pool activation — the main one. We plan to deploy via PoolFactoryV2 with two reserves (Aquarius LP as collateral, XLM borrowable) and fund the backstop ourselves. Is clearing the threshold the whole story, or is there a reward-zone / whitelist step that needs your side? And — more to the point — $13.3K of first-loss capital against a market that could hold ~$450K of equity looks thin to us. What size would you actually want to see before this is a market you are comfortable existing? We would rather size it to your answer than to the minimum.

Q2

LP share token as a collateral class. As far as we know this would be the first AMM LP token used as Blend collateral. We have now run the flow at c_factor 0.60 end-to-end; 0.50 works equally well, it just lowers the boundary. Does 0.50–0.60 look right to you, and is there anything else in the reserve config (max_util, supply cap, the IR curve) that should differ from a plain asset? We will take your number over ours.

Q3

Oracle. We intend to point the pool at our own adapter implementing the Blend oracle interface, pricing the LP from the pool invariant plus Reflector feeds — LP = 2·√(K·Pₜ·Pₖ)/L — with a deviation breaker that halts new borrowing on a stale or dislocated feed. Is a custom oracle per pool something you are comfortable with, and are there requirements we should meet (decimals, base asset, staleness semantics, prices vs lastprice)? Would you want that adapter in audit scope before you would consider the pool legitimate?

A finding worth passing on. When our oracle’s price entries expired under Soroban state TTL, every entrypoint reverted with an untyped host trap (VM call trapped: UnreachableCodeReached) rather than PoolError::InvalidPrice (#1210), and nothing in the trace named the oracle. A missing price and a bad price surface very differently; if #1210 covered the missing case it would save integrators a great deal of time.

Q4

Reserve timelock. For mainnet, is the intended pattern to configure every reserve during Setup before activation? Is there any supported path to add one later without the week’s wait, or is a fresh pool the answer?

Q5

Liquidation and bad debt. We are publishing an MIT-licensed reference liquidator that unwinds atomically (win auction → withdraw LP through Aquarius → swap one leg → repay), and running our own bot so auctions clear from day one. Given the collateral is an LP token rather than a listed asset: do you expect meaningful third-party liquidator interest, or should we assume we are the liquidator of last resort? We are willing, but would want an explicit ceiling rather than open-ended exposure. Any concern about concentration — early on, one vault position is effectively the whole market?

Q6

Interface stability. Are flash_loan and the exec_op(caller, token, amount, fee) receiver signature stable for the foreseeable future? We would rather know now than discover it at upgrade time.
