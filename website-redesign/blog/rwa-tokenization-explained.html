What Is RWA Tokenization? How Real-World Assets Move On-Chain

WhaleHub Research Last updated: September 2026 11 min read

RWA tokenization means issuing a blockchain token that represents a claim on something outside the blockchain — a Treasury bill, a building, a loan book. The technology is the easy part. What determines whether a tokenized asset is worth anything is the legal structure underneath it, and that is where most of this industry's difficulty, and most of its failures, actually live.

The short version

A token is a record. It is worth what someone is contractually obliged to give you in exchange for it, and worth nothing more. Tokenization changes how that claim is transferred and settled — faster, around the clock, programmable — but it does not create the claim, improve the asset, or reduce its risk.

So the question to ask about any RWA product is never "is it on-chain?" It is: who holds the asset, what exactly does my token entitle me to, and in which court do I enforce it?

How tokenization actually works

An asset is placed inside a legal entity — typically a special purpose vehicle, trust, or regulated fund. That entity issues units. A token is minted to represent those units, and a transfer agent maintains the link between token holders and the entity's register. Owning the token means owning a share of the entity, which owns the asset.

Four parties have to exist and keep existing for this to work:

  • The custodian holds the actual asset — the bonds at a prime broker, the deed at a title company, the loan documents in a vault.
  • The issuing entity owns the asset and issues the claims. Its jurisdiction determines your rights.
  • The transfer agent keeps the register of who owns what, and is the reason a token transfer is legally meaningful rather than just a ledger update.
  • The oracle or administrator publishes the asset's value on-chain so the token can be priced, redeemed or used as collateral.

Remove any one and the token degrades into an IOU from a company you have never met. This is the substance of the thing, and it is why "we tokenized X" is a claim about paperwork far more than about software.

What is actually being tokenized

Overwhelmingly, short-dated government debt. Tokenized Treasuries and money-market funds dominate real volume because the underlying is liquid, uniformly priced, already dematerialised and unexciting. Property and art generate most of the headlines and almost none of the flow.

Asset classWhy it works — or doesn't
Treasuries, money-market fundsLiquid, one obvious price, already electronic, redeemable daily. The natural first case, and where the volume is.
Private creditReal demand — the asset is illiquid and investors want an exit. But valuation is a model, not a market, and defaults are discovered late.
CommoditiesGold works because it is fungible and vaulting is a solved business. Most others are not fungible enough to tokenize usefully.
Real estatePopular in pitch decks. Each property is unique, valuation is an opinion, and transfer touches a land registry that does not care about your chain.
Art, collectiblesFractionalising a unique object creates a claim nobody can price and nobody can force a sale of. Mostly a liquidity mirage.

There is a pattern. Tokenization works best on assets that were already liquid and standardised — which is the opposite of the pitch usually made for it. It rarely turns an illiquid asset into a liquid one; it moves an already-liquid asset onto faster rails.

What tokenization genuinely improves

Setting the hype aside, four things are real:

  • Settlement speed. Traditional securities settle in a day or two. A token settles in seconds, and the capital tied up waiting is freed.
  • Operating hours. Markets close; blockchains do not. For an asset whose value accrues continuously, this matters more than it sounds.
  • Composability. A tokenized Treasury can be posted as collateral in a lending protocol without leaving custody. That is genuinely new, and it is the reason serious institutions are interested.
  • Smaller minimums. A fund with a six-figure minimum can be held in fractions. This widens access, though regulation often re-narrows it.

Notice what is missing: higher returns. Tokenization is a plumbing improvement. It reduces friction and cost. It does not make the underlying asset pay more, and any product implying otherwise is selling something else.

Why most projects stall

Because issuing a token takes days and building the legal and operational machinery around it takes years. Custody agreements, transfer agency, investor eligibility, redemption mechanics, and a regulator in every jurisdiction a holder lives in — that is the actual product, and it is not a software problem.

The recurring failure modes:

  • No redemption path. Tokens issued, no committed mechanism to convert them back into the asset or cash. The token trades at whatever the secondary market will bear, which for a thin market is a large discount.
  • Eligibility mismatch. The token is freely transferable on-chain but the underlying security legally is not, so transfers are either blocked by a whitelist — undermining the liquidity pitch — or happen anyway and create a compliance problem.
  • Valuation opacity. For anything other than a market-priced asset, the on-chain price comes from an administrator's model. If that model is not independently checked, the token's price is an assertion.
  • Jurisdiction roulette. The SPV sits somewhere convenient for the issuer, not somewhere convenient for you to sue.

How to judge an RWA product

Five questions, in order. If the first three have no clear answer, the rest does not matter.

  1. What exactly does the token entitle me to? A share in a fund, a debt claim, a beneficial interest in a trust? "Exposure to" is not an answer.
  2. Who holds the asset and who audits that? Named custodian, named auditor, published attestation with a date on it.
  3. How do I get out? Contractual redemption, or only a secondary market? If the latter, how deep is it on a bad day?
  4. Where does the price come from? A live market, or an administrator's NAV? How often, and checked by whom?
  5. Which jurisdiction, and can I actually use it? Eligibility rules may exclude you entirely — better to find out before depositing.

Tokenized assets sit under securities law in most major jurisdictions, which is why serious platforms run full identity and eligibility checks at onboarding rather than treating compliance as optional. The summary

Tokenization moves a claim onto faster, programmable rails. It does not improve the asset, raise its yield, or remove its risk, and it does not reliably make illiquid things liquid. The projects that work are the ones that did the unglamorous legal and custodial work first and treated the token as the last step rather than the first.

Frequently asked questions

What is RWA tokenization?

RWA tokenization is issuing a blockchain token that represents a claim on a real-world asset — a Treasury bill, a property, a loan, a commodity. The token is a record of ownership or entitlement; the asset itself stays in the physical or legal world, held by a custodian and governed by a legal structure. The token has value only to the extent that structure is enforceable.

What assets are actually being tokenized?

In practice, overwhelmingly short-dated government debt. Tokenized Treasuries and money-market funds dominate because the asset is liquid, uniformly priced, already dematerialised and boring — which is exactly what makes it easy to wrap. Private credit is second. Real estate and art attract the most headlines and the least volume, because valuation is subjective and transfer is slow.

Does owning the token mean you own the asset?

Usually not directly. Most structures put the asset in a special purpose vehicle, a trust, or a regulated fund, and the token represents a share in that entity or a contractual claim against it. Your recourse if something goes wrong runs through that entity and its jurisdiction, not through the blockchain. Read what the token is a claim on before assuming it is the asset.

Why do most RWA projects stall?

Because the hard part is not the token. It is custody, transfer agency, investor eligibility, redemption mechanics and the regulator in every jurisdiction where a holder lives. Issuing the token takes days; building the legal and operational machinery around it takes years, and that machinery is what a buyer is really pricing.

Is RWA tokenization regulated?

Yes, and typically as securities. A token representing debt, equity or a fund interest is generally a security in most major jurisdictions regardless of the technology, which brings issuance rules, disclosure obligations and investor eligibility limits. Most serious RWA platforms restrict access accordingly and run full KYC and AML checks.

What yield do tokenized RWAs pay?

Whatever the underlying asset pays, minus fees. A tokenized Treasury pays roughly the Treasury rate less the manager's cut; a tokenized private credit fund pays the loan book's return less fees and losses. There is no tokenization premium. If a tokenized RWA advertises a yield far above its underlying asset class, the excess is coming from leverage, token incentives, or undisclosed risk.
